Young Dro is making sure the 20-year anniversary of his classic debut album, Best Thang Smokin’, is being celebrated properly.

The Atlanta rap veteran has spent the past several weeks revisiting the 2006 project in a major way. Dro has been shooting new music videos for songs from the album that never received official visuals when the project originally dropped.

Now, Dro is back with another one.

The latest video is for “Hear Me Cry,” an underrated deep cut from Best Thang Smokin’. The song has received plenty of love from longtime fans, especially as Dro continues to bring attention back to an album that helped establish him as one of Atlanta’s most recognizable voices.

Young Dro Brings “Hear Me Cry” Back to Life

“Hear Me Cry” was never one of the biggest singles from Best Thang Smokin, but that has not stopped fans from appreciating the track all these years later.

Now, Dro is giving the song a visual for the first time.

The release continues a special anniversary run that has seen the rapper return to several songs from his 2006 debut. Rather than only celebrating the album through social media posts or performances, Dro has been creating brand-new visuals for songs that were overlooked when the album was originally released.

That has made the 20th anniversary celebration feel much bigger.

For longtime Dro fans, it is also an opportunity to revisit songs they may not have heard in years.

Dro Previously Released Videos for “We Lied” and “They Don’t Really Know Bout Dro”

“Hear Me Cry” follows other Best Thang Smokin tracks that Dro has recently filmed videos for.

advertisement

Over the past few weeks, he has released or worked on visuals for “We Lied” and “They Don’t Really Know Bout Dro.”

Both songs are part of the same effort to revisit the 2006 album and give some of its deeper cuts the visual treatment they never received back then.

The move has resonated with fans who have followed Dro since his early days.

In 2006, music videos were still a major part of how artists introduced songs to wider audiences. Not every album track received a visual, though, meaning some songs could remain hidden gems within a project’s tracklist.

Two decades later, Dro is changing that.

Fans Are Praising Young Dro’s Anniversary Run

Dro’s decision to revisit Best Thang Smokin has received strong reactions from fans.

Many have praised the Atlanta rapper for giving these older songs new life instead of simply focusing on newer material.

The approach also highlights just how much music is contained within Dro’s catalog.

For fans who have always considered Best Thang Smokin an important Atlanta rap album, seeing Dro return to songs like “Hear Me Cry” can feel like opening up an old part of the city’s music history.

The album arrived during an important period for Atlanta hip-hop.

Dro was already developing his reputation before the album, but Best Thang Smokin helped take his career to another level.

‘Best Thang Smokin’ Arrived in 2006

Best Thang Smokin was released in 2006 and became a major project in Young Dro’s career.

The album represented Dro’s emergence as a solo star and helped cement his place in the Atlanta rap scene.

His distinctive voice, Southern slang, charismatic delivery, and memorable hooks made him stand out.

The project also arrived during a period when Atlanta was becoming one of the most influential centers of hip-hop.

Artists from the city were dominating radio, clubs, mixtapes, and television. Dro became an important part of that movement.

Now, 20 years later, he is giving fans another reason to revisit that era.

Young Dro’s Atlanta Legacy Remains Strong

Dro has always represented Atlanta in his music.

His style is heavily connected to the city, from his slang to his delivery and the stories he tells.

That connection is still apparent in 2026.

The rapper continues to represent Atlanta while collaborating with both longtime peers and newer artists.

His latest anniversary videos also serve as a reminder of how much influence that era of Atlanta hip-hop continues to have.

For fans who discovered Dro through Best Thang Smokin, the anniversary celebration offers plenty of nostalgia.

For younger listeners, it provides an opportunity to discover some of his older material for the first time.

Dro Is Still Appearing on New Music in 2026

Even while looking back at his past, Young Dro has remained active with new music.

The Atlanta veteran has appeared on several 2026 releases, showing that he is still contributing to the culture rather than simply celebrating his history.

One recent appearance came on Trendsetter Sense’s “Lord Jesus,” which also features 1K Phew.

The collaboration gives Dro another opportunity to show that his distinctive Atlanta style still works alongside artists from different generations.

Dro was also featured on “Straight From The A,” a collaboration involving PSC, T.I., Big Kuntry King, and DC Young Fly.

That lineup brings together several names closely connected to Atlanta’s hip-hop culture.

Young Dro and T.I. Continue Their Atlanta Connection

Dro’s history with T.I. is especially important when discussing his career.

T.I. played a major role in helping introduce Dro to a larger audience, and Dro became one of the prominent artists associated with the Grand Hustle movement.

Their connection has remained important throughout the years.

The “Straight From The A” collaboration is another example of that Atlanta connection continuing into 2026.

With Dro, T.I., Big Kuntry King, and DC Young Fly all appearing on the record, the song brings together different generations of Atlanta talent.

It also fits naturally with Dro’s current anniversary celebration.

“Hear Me Cry” Highlights the Deep Cuts

One of the most exciting parts of Dro’s anniversary campaign is the focus on songs that might not immediately come to mind when discussing Best Thang Smokin.

“Hear Me Cry” is exactly that type of track.

While the album produced more recognizable records, deep cuts like this one helped make the project feel complete.

Dro returning to those songs shows that he remembers the entire album, not just the biggest moments.

It also gives fans who have always championed those tracks something to celebrate.

20 Years Later, Dro Is Still Giving Fans New Content

Twenty years is a long time in hip-hop.

Artists can go through multiple eras, sounds, trends, and generations during that span.

Yet Young Dro continues to find ways to make his catalog relevant.

His decision to create new videos for Best Thang Smokin tracks demonstrates how much confidence he still has in the music.

Instead of allowing the album to remain a piece of Atlanta rap history, Dro is actively bringing it back into the conversation.

Each new visual gives another song from the project a chance to reach longtime listeners and a new generation.

Young Dro’s 2006 Classic Gets a New Chapter

The 20th anniversary of Best Thang Smokin has become much more than a simple anniversary celebration.

With videos for “We Lied,” “They Don’t Really Know Bout Dro,” and now “Hear Me Cry,” Dro is expanding the visual history of his debut album two decades after its release.

Fans have responded by praising him for revisiting these songs and giving them the attention they did not receive in 2006.

At the same time, Dro has continued appearing on new music, including Trendsetter Sense’s “Lord Jesus” with 1K Phew and “Straight From The A” with PSC, T.I., Big Kuntry King, and DC Young Fly.

That combination of honoring the past while remaining active in the present makes Dro’s 20th-anniversary run especially notable.

As the celebration continues, fans will undoubtedly be watching to see which Best Thang Smokin deep cut gets the visual treatment next.
